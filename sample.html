1 · The device and its rule

A Class II device with a 510(k) path

Pulse oximeters sit under one regulation in FDA's cardiovascular panel. New ones reach the US market through a 510(k), by showing they are substantially equivalent to a device already cleared.

An oximeter is a device used to transmit radiation at a known wavelength(s) through blood and to measure the blood oxygen saturation based on the amount of reflected or scattered radiation.21 CFR 870.2700(a) · Class II (performance standards)

2 · Clearances and review time

Steady clearances, slower reviews

FDA clears a steady stream of oximeters every year, but the median time from submission to decision has risen sharply since 2020.

For planning: budget for the recent review times, not the long-run average, and expect questions on accuracy across skin tones, which FDA has emphasised in its recent pulse oximeter guidance.

7 · Regulatory roadmap

The path to a US clearance

An outline roadmap for a new oximeter. Each step cites the rule or standard behind it.

Confirm the classificationProduct code DQA under 21 CFR 870.2700, Class II: a premarket notification is required (21 CFR 807 Subpart E).

Choose and vet predicatesPick cleared devices with the same intended use and technology, and check each one's recalls and adverse events.

Plan performance testingBench and clinical accuracy testing to ISO 80601-2-61, following FDA's pulse oximeter guidance, including performance across skin pigmentation.

Electrical safety, EMC and alarmsIEC 60601-1, IEC 60601-1-2 and, for alarm functions, IEC 60601-1-8.

Software and risk filesSoftware lifecycle evidence to IEC 62304; risk management to ISO 14971.

Quality systemDesign controls and supplier controls under the Quality Management System Regulation (21 CFR 820), which incorporates ISO 13485.

9 · How this was made

Sources and limits

FDA figures come from the official 510(k), recall and MAUDE files (openFDA), device identifiers from AccessGUDID, and regulation text from the eCFR, refreshed as FDA publishes. Patent classes are from the Cooperative Patent Classification scheme. Counts depend on how FDA codes each record; a device coded under another product code is not counted here.
