Case study · measurement

Measuring glucose in blood

8 research questionsFDA product codes NBW, PZI, CGA, MDS, QBJ, NCT and moreCPC A61B5/14532

Glucose is the most measured analyte in medicine: in the central laboratory, at the bedside, by people with diabetes several times a day, and now continuously by sensors under the skin. This case asks how each kind of measurement works, how accurate it has to be, why a non-invasive method has been so hard to bring to market, and what the public record shows about the devices.

      7 · How this case was researched

      Open-source tools on one research server

      1. Search. Each question was researched on the open web through Vane, a private search engine (Perplexica with SearXNG), with a local model.
      2. Read. Every cited page was read in full (trafilatura, Docling). References in an offline encyclopedia and the Priora knowledge base were followed to the papers they cite, read from a local copy of PubMed; the encyclopedia itself is never cited.
      3. Check. The drafts were split into single claims, and each claim was checked against the source text with a local model and nomic-embed-text embeddings.
      4. Write. Findings were written from checked claims and figures only, and each was reread against its own sources before publication.

      FDA, adverse-event (MAUDE), 21 CFR and patent-class data come from the Priora database. No step used a cloud AI service.
